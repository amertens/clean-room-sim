## Summary

The package is already smaller than causalRisk by the measure a user meets: cleanTMLE 0.5.0 has 12 exports against 26 function names in causalRisk's user-facing reference. Most of its 3,068 lines of R are code the outcome-blind design depends on: the shared fitter, cross-fitting, the check for tuning settings chosen at the edge of their grid, the lock and design-report hashes, and the blinding in `unblind()`. The cuts that survive review remove about 400 lines of R and two packages from Imports, mostly the time-to-event path, the weighting and matching comparators, dead methods and compatibility code (recommendations 8 to 14). The larger gain in parsimony is in what a new user meets first, the 22 arguments of `analysis_plan()` and the nine fields of the variable dictionary, which recommendations 9, 13 and 15 make smaller or easier to learn.

The package is not a thin wrapper and cannot become one before the paper. It delegates the targeting of the ATE, trimmed ATE and ATT to tmle, but it owns its learner library, its cross-fitting, its super-learner combination and its TMLE for the overlap-weighted effect. Handing the fitter to SuperLearner or sl3 would change every design-stage number, cost two to four times the fits (`02-wrapper.md:30-38`), and weaken the guarantee that the simulation tests exactly the estimator run after unblinding, so the paper and vignettes should not call it a thin wrapper. It can be made trustworthy as it stands: pin each in-house component to the established package it stands in for, against stored reference values, and record the build and the dependency versions that every estimate relies on (recommendations 4 and 7). Letting a plan name an external fitter is the route to a stricter wrapper after the paper (recommendation 34).

The simulation has produced no evidence yet, while the case study already has results worth showing. On the simulation side, none of the 711 registry keys comes from the simulation study, and the pilot's contrast step never ran. Its claims and decision rules should be written into the plan before that step is read, every planned contrast kept in its table, and a contrast the budget cannot resolve reported as unresolved with its claim rescoped (recommendation 2). In the case study, every result so far is free of the outcome. The protocol's comparison has poor overlap for the ATE, concentrated in the comparison arm. The comparison between operators is the one that passes the rule written into the plan, and its estimator was chosen by simulation, although the simulation could not separate the two candidates. And at four months the comparison between operators had no patient outside the overlap band and a largest weight of about 4.2 (`first_four_months_operators_n_outside`, `first_four_months_operators_ate_max_weight`), yet the simulation found no feasible estimand, with about 35 and 21 expected events in the two arms (`first_four_months_operators_expected_events_treated_min`, `first_four_months_operators_expected_events_control_min`). That result is the clearest demonstration in the project of what an outcome-blind simulation sees and an overlap diagnostic does not. The largest risk is ahead: the scripts that run after unblinding are not ready, and they should be fixed, committed and rehearsed on synthetic outcomes in a separate folder before the outcome store is built (recommendation 1).

The paper's claim has to be rescoped before it can persuade. The drafts are accurate and candid but argue for one component. The literature survey finds a published source for each part of the workflow: staging with a clean room [@muntner2024], outcome-blind simulation to choose the estimator [@nanceCausalRoadmapSimulations2024a] with a trial version and a software tool since, and diagnostics read before unblinding in a large safety program. It finds no work that uses an outcome-blind simulation to decide which estimand a design can deliver, and no package that locks the plan and the estimator before the outcome. The broad claim that running the causal roadmap with TMLE inside a clean room is itself an advance is therefore not supported, and under the project's rule the claim is rescoped rather than defended. The claim that survives, and that can be argued with force, is that this work extends outcome-blind simulation from the choice of the estimator to the choice of the estimand, turns the verdict into a decision through an order fixed in advance, tests how often the verdict is right, and provides software in which the estimator applied after the outcome is joined is the one the simulation evaluated (recommendation 5). Regulators now ask that the analysis plan state the estimand, its feasibility and its anticipated operating characteristics, and the design-stage tables supply exactly that before any outcome is seen. The first page should also carry the limits: a verdict that holds over the simulated family, changes made detectable rather than impossible, no remedy for unmeasured confounding, the fact that withholding the outcome does not prevent bias when the outcome is predictable from the covariates the design stage uses [cite: Fisher 2020, blinding outcomes is neither necessary nor sufficient, arXiv:2007.02514], and analysts who had seen the outcomes.

## The five priorities

They are ranked by what is lost if they are skipped: the first protects a property that cannot be recovered once outcomes exist, the second keeps the paper's evidence from looking tuned, the third keeps the case study's estimator selection from looking tuned, the fourth makes the numerical code trustworthy before anyone changes it, and the fifth makes the paper argue only for what its evidence carries. Each survived both reviews only with changes, and the text below is the revised version.

### 1. Make every step after unblinding fixed, checkable and rehearsed before the outcome store is built

For the paper (case study). What it protects, stated as narrowly as the reviewers asked, is that the re-execution's plan and code did not change after its outcome store existed, and that the primary estimator applied is the one the simulation evaluated (`manuscript/methods.qmd:71`). The broader outcome-blind claim is already limited to the software withholding the outcome (`writing/brief.md:32`; `manuscript/methods.qmd:200`).

The scripts that run after unblinding are not ready. Script 25 loops over every lock in alphabetical order with no error handler (`rescueCo/scripts/25_estimate.R:29-41`), and `unblind()` refuses a lock whose design report has no feasible estimand or a `STOP` negative-control verdict (`cleanTMLE/R/unblind.R:7-11`, `:94-98`). The protocol comparisons sort first, so a refusal there would stop the run before the confirmatory comparison is estimated. Whether they will be refused is not yet known, because only the comparison between operators has finished in the final cohort. Script 25 also passes only the primary outcome (`:33`), runs no comparator although the Methods promise one (`:36`; `manuscript/methods.qmd:120`), leaves the longitudinal TMLE as a comment (`:44-52`), and writes its estimates and logs into `rescueCo/results/reexec_050/` (`:25`, `:38`, `:42`), the folder treated as outcome-free and read by `writing/export_results.R:13`. ### 4. Test and pin the in-house numerical code before any cut or learner change

For a 0.5.x release. Keeping the fitter in-house survived both reviews: one fit per fold yields a whole boosting or elastic-net path (`cleanTMLE/R/learners.R:123-147`), bootstrap copies share folds (`learners.R:117-121`), and SuperLearner's wrappers differ in fold draws, leaf sizes, net settings and an inner `cv.glmnet` (`02-wrapper.md:28-36`). What is missing is anything that ties the fitter to the packages it stands in for. No test calls `.fit_Q()`; the edge check is tested on boosted trees only (`cleanTMLE/tests/testthat/test-learners.R:53-65`); no test fits the neural net or the default library, because the fixtures use `ps_library = "glm"` (`helper-fixtures.R:19`, `:64`); the overlap-weighted TMLE is checked on one dataset with the true nuisance functions (`test-fit-candidate.R:39-55`), not for coverage; and tmle has no version floor (`cleanTMLE/DESCRIPTION:28`).

Add a direct test of `.fit_Q()` (cross-fitting and the treatment column), glmnet and nnet cases for `.edge_check()`, and one test that fits the default library. Pin `.nnloglik()` to `SuperLearner::method.CC_nloglik` on the minimized log-loss rather than the weights, which are poorly determined among the library's 17 members, several of them near-duplicates (`test-learners.R:9-11`). Pin the glmnet and xgboost paths to `cv.glmnet(foldid = )` and `xgb.cv(folds = )` on the same folds, comparing the selected point of each path. Pin the targeting of the ATE and ATT to stored reference values on a fixed dataset, and the ATE also to a short independent logistic fluctuation, with tmle's refit of the propensity score for the ATT both triggered and not; the existing check at `test-fit-candidate.R:119-134` calls tmle a second time with the same arguments, so it moves with tmle and could not have caught the change in 2.1.1. Check the coverage of the overlap-weighted TMLE with the true nuisance functions. Require `tmle (>= 2.1.1)`, pass the targeting argument that 2.1.1 changed explicitly in `.tmle_args()` (`cleanTMLE/R/fit_candidate.R:48-61`; the installed help names it `target.gwt`), and remove the `formals()` guard.

The synthesis also proposed a test that runs the design stage under a permuted outcome. It is dropped, because in 0.5.0 no design-stage function receives an outcome, the lock refuses the plan's outcome columns (`cleanTMLE/R/lock.R:85-88`, tested at `test-lock.R:1-5`), and `test-blinding.R` checks that no design object holds one, so the test would pass by construction. The real exposure cannot be tested by the package: it cannot tell whether an undeclared design column carries outcome information, and the lock keeps the outcome-observation indicators (`lock.R:132-134`), which the ledger records as informative about the outcome (`rescueCo/BLINDING_LEDGER.md:26`, `:43`). The vignette and the paper should say that the design data's freedom from the outcome rests on the steward's build of it. They should also say which parts of the package are in-house and which are delegated, and they should not describe the package as a thin wrapper.

Evidence: as cited; `06-check-guarantees.md` section 4; `07-check-framing.md` recommendation 4; `01-parsimony.md:273`; `02-wrapper.md:211`. Effort: one to two days. Risk: none to results; a failing pin is a finding. ### 5. Rescope the paper's claim to the estimand, with the workflow as its setting, after reading the nearest work

For the paper (writing). The brief, the introduction and the vignette present one component, choosing the estimand by outcome-blind simulation, and the introduction never gives TMLE as a reason for the design (`writing/brief.md:14`, `:21`; `manuscript/introduction.qmd:27-29`). The synthesis proposed the joined workflow (roadmap, TMLE and clean room together) as the headline. The framing review refuted that headline using the literature survey, which finds a source for each part: staging with a clean room [@muntner2024]; outcome-blind simulation, run after data collection and before effect estimation, to choose the estimator of a TMLE [@nanceCausalRoadmapSimulations2024a], with a trial version that prespecifies a data-adaptive plan and tests it by plasmode simulation [cite: Balzer 2026, DOI 10.1177/17407745261417227] and a tool that compares estimators for a prespecified ATE [cite: Meng and Huang, REFINE2, DOI 10.1093/aje/kwaf195]; a targeted-learning analysis plan [@gruber2022]; and prespecified diagnostics read before unblinding [cite: Conover 2025, DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocae317], which a safety program applied at scale, withholding the evaluations that failed them [cite: Haynes 2026, DOI 10.1002/pds.70435]. The gap the survey supports concerns the estimand: in the uses it found, the estimand is fixed before the simulation is run (`08-literature.md`, section 6). The synthesis's fallback gap, that no check made before the outcome tests whether an estimator delivers the estimand, is contradicted by the same papers.

The evidence also limits what the workflow claim could rest on. The simulation's main grid has one candidate (`simulation/R/map.R:14`, `:21`), so the choice of estimator has no simulation evidence; the clean-room part is a software property with no evidence of bias prevented (`05-writing.md:184`); and the case study's approver had seen the outcomes (`2026-10-18-outcome-blind-design-stage.md:129`). The workflow can be described and argued as the setting, while the evidence speaks to the estimand verdict and to choosing before rather than after the outcome.

First add, through sci-cite, the near work the survey lists with verified identifiers, at least Barnard 2026, Conover 2025, Liu 2025, Karim 2026, Balzer 2026, Meng and Huang, Haynes 2026 and Fisher 2020 (`lit/zotero_to_add.tsv`), and read them. Then Andrew chooses the headline. The one the literature supports reads: outcome-blind simulation has been used to choose the estimator before effect estimation [@nanceCausalRoadmapSimulations2024a], and staging with a clean room restricts access to the outcome [@muntner2024]; this paper uses the simulation to judge which estimand a design can deliver, makes the verdict a decision through an order fixed in advance, tests how often the verdict is right, and provides software in which the primary estimator applied after the outcome is joined is the one the simulation evaluated. That is still an advance, and it can be argued with force on two points the survey supplies. Recent guidance from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) asks the analysis plan to state the estimand, its feasibility and its anticipated operating characteristics [cite: FDA 2024 draft guidance on non-interventional studies; ICH guideline M14], and the design-stage tables are that evidence, produced before the outcome. And the diagnostic gates in use today withhold the effect estimate but read observed outcome counts and negative-control estimates [cite: Conover 2025], whereas cleanTMLE's design objects hold no outcome at all.

Two software conditions go with the claim.
