# Audit 04: the Rescue.Co case study

Written 22 October 2026. This is a document of proposals for Andrew; nothing in the package, the paper, the vignettes or the specs has been changed.

I read the blinding ledger, both specs, scripts 20 to 25, the digest, the registry, the exported tables, the design-stage tables, the rendered design report for the comparison between operators (it sits in the allowed results folder), the outline and proposed results, and the supervisor log. I opened no outcome file and no file in the legacy results folder, started no R process, and did not read the unblinded analysis. I also read cleanTMLE's R code where a claim depends on it, with git read-only.

The short answer is that the re-execution already supports three specific, checkable and outcome-free statements. For the ATE, the protocol's comparison has poor overlap at four months and at the end, almost entirely because of the comparison arm, while the Rescue.Co arm itself is well supported. The comparison between operators is the only comparison that passes a rule committed before the design reports existed. And the estimator for that comparison was picked by a simulation, not by an analyst. The re-execution does not yet support the statement that the design stage refused to estimate the protocol's comparison, because the simulations for the poor-overlap comparisons have not finished and because the protocol's own estimand, the ATT, was ruled out by the plan's choice of target and not by a lack of support. It cannot support the statement that main-terms TMLE is better than the super learner, because the simulation could not separate them. The largest risks lie ahead of the numbers: scripts 24 and 25 have never run, script 25 would stop at its first refusal, the learner-defaults item could prompt a rerun that reads as tuning, and the rule "fixed in advance" was written by people who had seen the unblinded estimates.

On the state of the run, `rescueCo/logs/supervisor.txt:1-2` shows a supervisor started at 20:53 on 22 October that launched the first-four-months comparison of Rescue.Co with arrival by other means on two workers. In `rescueCo/results/reexec_050/` only the comparison between operators has a design report in each run (`final/L3_dossier.html`, `first4m/L3_dossier.html`; the specs call these dossiers); the other comparisons have design assessments only. So simulation, negative-control and selection results exist for one comparison per run, and everything else is overlap and population results. I cannot tell from the files I may open how far the remaining locks have progressed.

### The protocol's four-month check

The protocol's check used the first four months of enrolment (19 February to 18 June 2024). According to the approved inventory it was an overlap and retention check with no estimation rule (`rescueCo/deviations_inventory.csv` row 21). It measured the share of Rescue.Co patients left unmatched, and it passed, with 80% matched through June 2024; its propensity model included scene care, prior care, signs of life, payment and distance, which are recorded at or after the transport being compared (row 2, notes). The re-execution ran all five comparisons on that window, with 1,665 patients enrolled (`first_four_months_count_patients`), using a propensity score restricted to covariates fixed before transport. The cohorts are the final data cut restricted by enrolment date, without the complete-case restriction because no follow-up existed in 2024 (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-rescueco-reexecution-0.5.0.md:73, 105`).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rescue.Co vs all other transport, all arrivals | 48.9% | 41.6% | 48.3% | 53.7% |
| Rescue.Co vs all other transport, direct arrivals | 33.5% | 29.9% | 63.3% | 64.6% |
| Rescue.Co vs arrival by other means | 37.8% | 30.2% | 58.0% | 63.1% |
| Any ambulance vs arrival by other means | 14.5% | 10.8% | 82.4% | 85.2% |
| Rescue.Co vs other ambulance operators | 0.0% | 0.2% | 100.0% | 99.6% |

The columns are `<run>_<comparison>_share_outside_band` and `<run>_<comparison>_trimmed_share_kept_comparator`. Each comparison has the same overlap grade at four months as in the final cohort, and the shares outside the band moved by 4 to 8 points as enrolment grew (arithmetic on the table), which argues that poor overlap reflects who is carried by an ambulance and not a small sample.

By arm, the poor grade comes from the comparison arm. For the protocol's comparison over direct arrivals, `final_protocol_direct_n_outside_comparator` of `final_protocol_direct_n_comparator` comparison patients lie outside the band, against `final_protocol_direct_n_outside_treated` of `final_protocol_direct_n_treated` Rescue.Co patients; at four months the pairs are `first_four_months_protocol_direct_n_outside_comparator` of `first_four_months_protocol_direct_n_comparator` and `first_four_months_protocol_direct_n_outside_treated` of `first_four_months_protocol_direct_n_treated`. Trimming keeps `first_four_months_protocol_direct_trimmed_share_kept_treated` of the Rescue.Co arm at four months. The 2024 result (Rescue.Co patients can be matched) and the re-execution (much of the comparison arm has no Rescue.Co counterpart) therefore do not contradict each other. They answer different questions: the first bears on the effect among the patients Rescue.Co carried, which is the matched ATT the protocol specified, and the second bears on the ATE for the whole cohort.

For the comparison between operators the four-month cohort had `first_four_months_operators_n` patients (`first_four_months_operators_n_treated` and `first_four_months_operators_n_comparator`), a cross-fitted c-statistic of `first_four_months_operators_c_statistic` and no patient outside the band. No estimand was feasible. The main-terms candidate had a worst-case bias of `first_four_months_operators_sim_main_terms_worst_bias_min` to `first_four_months_operators_sim_main_terms_worst_bias_max`, and for the ATE its mean model SE (`first_four_months_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_se_mean_min` to `_max`) was far below the empirical SE (`first_four_months_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_emp_se_min` to `_max`), with coverage `first_four_months_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_coverage_min` to `_max`. The super learner met the bias and coverage tolerances, but its half-width, `first_four_months_operators_sim_super_learner_halfwidth_min` to `_max`, exceeded the tolerance of 0.055 (`22_design_stage.R:134`). Four of five negative controls were inestimable (`first_four_months_operators_nc_inestimable`).

The paper can claim that, on the data as later cut and with the thresholds in the 2026 plan, the protocol's comparison would have been graded poor for the ATE at the protocol's checkpoint, with the shortfall in the comparison arm; that the 2024 check measured something else; and that a confirmatory estimate for the comparison between operators was out of reach at that sample size. It would be an overclaim to say the design stage would have caught the problem in 2024. The plan was written in October 2026 by people who had seen the unblinded estimates (ledger lines 11-16), the data are a later cut (call 11 of the spec), and the propensity model differs from the 2024 one. The plan's retention threshold of 80% of each arm also coincides with the 80% of Rescue.Co patients that the 2024 check matched; the plan gives no source for it, so say where it came from.

Two further cautions apply to the operators result. First, the four-month failure of the main-terms candidate is largely an events-per-covariate problem, not a verdict on simple models. At four months the simulation expected about 56 to 70 events in total (`first_four_months_operators_expected_events_treated_min` to `_max` plus the control pair) against `first_four_months_count_covariate_columns` covariate columns, roughly one event per column; in the final cohort it expected about 305 to 391 events against `final_count_covariate_columns` columns, roughly four to five (arithmetic). An unpenalized logistic regression with one event per column is a poor stand-in for "a simple model". Second, the cross-fitted c-statistic rose from `first_four_months_operators_c_statistic` to `final_operators_c_statistic` as the cohort grew from `first_four_months_operators_n` to `final_operators_n` patients. The four-month near-chance value may partly reflect an unstable propensity score in 226 patients, so "the operators looked balanced at four months" is not a safe sentence (inference).

### The population gate

The plan limits how far an estimand below the ATE may move the target population: it must keep at least 80% of each arm and shift none of five named covariates by more than 0.25 standard deviations, and the overlap-weighted effect counts only for comparisons between operators (spec line 81; `23_registration_bundle.R:24-28`). In the final cohort every estimand below the ATE fails for both versions of the protocol's comparison and for Rescue.Co versus arrival by other means. Trimming keeps `final_protocol_all_arrivals_trimmed_share_kept_comparator`, `final_protocol_direct_trimmed_share_kept_comparator` and `final_rescueco_vs_other_means_trimmed_share_kept_comparator` of the comparison arm, and the largest shifts run from `final_protocol_direct_ato_max_shift` to `final_protocol_all_arrivals_trimmed_ate_max_shift`. For any ambulance versus arrival by other means only the trimmed ATE passes (`final_ambulance_vs_other_means_trimmed_share_kept_comparator`, shift `final_ambulance_vs_other_means_trimmed_ate_max_shift`; the ATT shift is `final_ambulance_vs_other_means_att_max_shift`). Between operators every estimand passes, with a largest shift of `final_operators_att_max_shift`. Four months gives the same pass and fail pattern, but the trimmed ATE for any ambulance passes narrowly there: `first_four_months_ambulance_vs_other_means_trimmed_ate_max_shift` against 0.25, and `first_four_months_ambulance_vs_other_means_trimmed_share_kept_comparator` against 80%.

The gate does little work in picking the confirmatory comparison. The rule tests the overlap grade first, and the gate applies to the primary estimand, which is the ATE whenever the ATE is feasible and passes by construction (`23_registration_bundle.R:34-35, 70-72`). The two protocol comparisons were excluded by their grade alone. The gate's visible job is to refuse estimands that move the population, which is what the unblinded analysis did not do: it reported trimmed-population estimates and then judged them not exchangeable (`rescueCo/reexec_vs_unblinded_2026-10-20.md:24`).

The place where the case study is most exposed to a knowledgeable reviewer is the ATT. The gate compares each estimand's population with the full cohort (`23_registration_bundle.R:36-37`; `cleanTMLE/R/assess_design.R:35-52`), and the ATT's population is the Rescue.Co patients, who differ from a cohort that is mostly not Rescue.Co by `final_protocol_direct_att_max_shift` standard deviations. Whether the comparison arm can represent those patients is a different question, and the design report answers it with the effective sample size of the comparison arm under ATT weights. That figure is `final_protocol_direct_att_ess_comparator` for the protocol's comparison over direct arrivals, larger than its `final_protocol_direct_n_treated` Rescue.Co patients, and `final_operators_att_ess_comparator` between operators, smaller than its `final_operators_n_treated` Rescue.Co patients. By that measure the protocol's own estimand is better supported in the protocol's comparison than in the comparison between operators (reading the keys). The plan's target is the whole cohort (spec 2026-10-18, line 58, the effect of transport in injured patients for whom a transport choice was made), and the ladder puts the ATE first and the ATT third (`22_design_stage.R:130`). That reverses the protocol, which specified the matched ATT, a change the unblinded analysis made on 4 April 2026 with estimates available (inventory row 2). The re-execution inherits the ordering and does not test it. I do not propose changing the gate after seeing these tables, which would be the adjustment the project rules forbid; I propose rescoping the claim, as set out below.

The paper can claim that, with thresholds fixed before the design reports, no estimand that moves the population away from the whole cohort is acceptable for three of five comparisons, and that the ATE for those comparisons rests on a comparison arm much of which lies outside the band. It would be an overclaim to say the gate protects validity. Passing it means five named covariates moved little and most of each arm remains; it says nothing about the other covariates or about unmeasured ones. It would be an overclaim to say the stage refused the protocol's comparison, because the simulation verdicts for those locks have not finished, and because the ATT was refused by the plan's target and not by lack of support. If the ATE comes out feasible for a poor-overlap comparison (largest ATE weights `final_protocol_all_arrivals_ate_max_weight` and `final_protocol_direct_ate_max_weight`), the plan allows it as a primary estimand there, and only the overlap grade keeps it from being confirmatory. Finally, "population gate" is a coined label (Andrew's rule against coined terms); describe it as a prespecified limit on how far the target population may move.

### The confirmatory comparison chosen by a rule fixed in advance

The rule takes the first of the five comparisons, in the stated order, whose overlap is good or moderate, whose primary estimand passes the gate, and whose negative-control verdict is not `STOP` (spec line 83; `23_registration_bundle.R:68-74`). The package gives `STOP` to a control whose interval lies entirely outside the null band and `FLAG` to an inconclusive one. In the final cohort the two protocol comparisons are graded poor, the comparison between operators is graded good (`final_operators_share_outside_band`, `final_operators_ate_max_weight`), its primary estimand is the ATE, and its verdict is FLAG. It is picked, and no later design report can change that because it precedes the other two in the order (`writing/digest.md:27`).

The ordering evidence is good for the thresholds. The plan and scripts were committed at 15:14 on 20 October (commit 55c5d81), and the lock for the comparison between operators was created at 22:11 Pacific time (design report header, `Lock created 2026-10-21T05:11:57Z`). The plan parameters in the working tree match the commit; the uncommitted diff changes only the `reviewers` text, the one-lock argument and the check mode (`git diff -- rescueCo/scripts/22_design_stage.R`). The script that built the lock is nonetheless not itself in git, so commit it before citing it.

The pick is not sensitive to the grade cut-off over a wide range (reading the exported tables, arithmetic). It holds for any cut-off for "poor" between 0.2% and 29.9%, the share outside the band for the protocol's comparison over direct arrivals. A cut-off above 29.9% would make that comparison eligible, though only if its ATE were also feasible in the pending simulation. The 5% and 20% cut-offs are the package's (`cleanTMLE/R/assess_design.R:111`) and the inventory records that they have no stated basis (`deviations_inventory.csv` row 21).

The paper can claim that the comparison was picked by a rule that used no outcome and reproduces the choice the unblinded analysis made while estimates existed (inventory row 1), and that the choice is stable over a range of cut-offs. It would be an overclaim to say the rule found the right comparison independently. The order places the comparison the unblinded analysis chose third, the ladder puts the ATE first as the unblinded analysis did, the thresholds were set after the team had seen the unblinded estimates, and a different author might have written a different rule. "Reproduces" is honest; "validates" is not. The Methods draft says the rule sits in a "registered plan" (`manuscript/methods.qmd:174`), which the governance decision rules out: registered now means written into the plan document (spec 2026-10-18, line 129). The identification table inside every lock still says "registered before unblinding" about the bias analysis for deaths before arrival (`22_design_stage.R:86-88`), so the supplement must explain that word once; changing it would change the lock hash.

The rule also changes the question, and the paper should say so. The protocol asked whether Rescue.Co transport beats other transport. The confirmatory comparison asks whether it beats other ambulance operators, among direct arrivals whose six-month status was recorded (`final_operators_n`). It cannot say whether an ambulance helps. The expected interval half-width, `final_operators_ate_selected_halfwidth`, is also close to the smallest difference the protocol was powered for, 0.055 (spec line 79), so the comparison can at best just separate an effect of that size from zero (inference from two numbers; a power figure would be new arithmetic).

### Main-terms TMLE selected over the super learner, and the learner-defaults item

For the ATE between operators the two libraries compare as follows.

| ATE, comparison between operators | Four months, main terms | Four months, super learner | Final, main terms | Final, super learner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Worst-case absolute bias | 0.050 to 0.059 | 0.023 to 0.024 | 0.011 | 0.018 |
| Coverage | 0.53 to 0.69 | 0.94 to 0.96 | 0.92 to 0.93 | 0.88 to 0.93 |
| Mean model SE | 0.073 to 0.079 | 0.063 to 0.066 | 0.025 to 0.026 | 0.024 to 0.026 |
| Empirical SE | 0.166 to 0.235 | 0.059 to 0.061 | 0.027 to 0.028 | 0.025 to 0.028 |
| Mean half-width | 0.143 to 0.156 | 0.123 to 0.129 | 0.0493 to 0.0518 | 0.0474 to 0.0506 |
| Unresolved cells of 8 | 8 | 6 | 8 | 8 |
| Datasets per outcome model | 100 | 100 | 300 | 300 |

The rows come from `<run>_operators_sim_<library>_ate_<quantity>` with library `main_terms` or `super_learner` and quantity `worst_bias`, `coverage`, `se_mean`, `emp_se`, `halfwidth` and `unresolved_cells`, plus `<run>_operators_sim_datasets_ate`. In the final cohort both libraries sit well inside the bias tolerance of 0.0275 (`22_design_stage.R:134`). The selection came from coverage alone: only the main-terms candidates passed on point estimates (minimum coverage `final_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_coverage_min` against `final_operators_sim_super_learner_ate_coverage_min`). The Wilson intervals overlap broadly (`final_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_coverage_lower_min` to `final_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_coverage_upper_max`, and `final_operators_sim_super_learner_ate_coverage_lower_min` to `final_operators_sim_super_learner_ate_coverage_upper_max`), and all `final_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_cells` cells of each library stayed unresolved at the cap (`final_operators_sim_main_terms_ate_unresolved_cells`, `final_operators_sim_super_learner_ate_unresolved_cells`). The truncation level is immaterial: `final_operators_n_outside` patients lie outside the band, so the selected 0.05 (`final_operators_ate_selected_truncation`) binds on at most two comparison patients.

What was selected is a TMLE with a main-terms logistic outcome regression and a super learner propensity score. The plan sets `ps_library = "default"` for both candidates and varies only the outcome library (`22_design_stage.R:132-133`; `cleanTMLE/R/fit_candidate.R:164-167`). Call it that, and not "main-terms TMLE" or "parametric TMLE".

Judged with the supervised-learning skill, the library is not the obvious culprit. It has one learner type from each of the four families (elastic net, multivariate adaptive regression splines, boosted trees and a small neural net), and the boosting setup is the skill's recipe: depth 1 to 5, learning rates 0.1 and 0.3, a cap of 500 trees and no minimum child weight (`cleanTMLE/R/learners.R:6-12, 78-79`). Four things fail the skill's checks or cannot be checked.

First, the neural net's decay sits at the bottom of its grid in five of five cross-fitting folds for the real propensity score (edge table in the design report), in more than two thirds of the simulated fits, and at the top of the grid in just under a third (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-outcome-blind-design-stage.md:145`). That is a consistent edge, so the skill requires the plateau test. The test needs each setting's cross-validated risk and its standard error, and the code stores only the mean (`learners.R:146, 149-156`). Network width is fixed at 5 with no recorded reason (`learners.R:11`). The pattern of extremes chosen and the middle almost never is not what a plateau looks like, since a flat risk would spread the choices; non-convergence at `maxit = 200` is a candidate explanation (`learners.R:71-73`), and the code does not record convergence (inference).

Second, the simulation keeps the edge counts but discards the per-setting risks and stacking weights (`simulate_design.R:317, 390`), so neither the plateau test nor the question "does the net get any weight?" can be answered without refitting. If the net's weight is near zero, its edge does not affect the verdict.

Third, the spec hopes that leaving the treatment unpenalized removes the super learner's extra bias (question 2). The treatment enters the outcome regression as one more column that the elastic net shrinks and that trees may rarely split on (`fit_candidate.R:38-46`). But that mechanism predicts a bias toward zero, while the reported bias is positive and every simulated surface has a positive effect (log odds ratio 0.5 in the plan table of the design report). The sign per surface should be checked before a fix is designed (inference).

Fourth, the shared positive bias (question 3) fits residual confounding: every simulated outcome model loads half on the propensity index (`simulate_design.R:16`), and the world's propensity score is a single in-sample fit (`assess_design.R:103`), which no cross-fitted score can recover exactly. If so, the design stage's bias numbers describe that simulated world and carry no sign for the real data (inference).

How this relates to the open item: the item's evidence is this design report, and its fixes could change which candidate the rule selects. If the super learner then passes, the rule picks the passing candidate with the lower worst-case root mean squared error (`simulate_design.R:238`), and that error is nearly tied (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-outcome-blind-design-stage.md:137`), so the selection would again be decided by noise. The selection is fragile in both directions, so the paper should not depend on which candidate wins. Guard the item against the one thing the project rules forbid, which is changing the design until a claim survives. Fixes should be judged on simulated data outside Rescue.Co (the simulation study's data-generating processes, as question 5 already proposes), because the Rescue.Co plasmode is built from these design data even though it contains no outcome.

The paper can claim that the simulation selected the estimator before any outcome, that both candidates were within the bias tolerance, that the data could not separate them on coverage, and that at four months the same rule would have chosen differently because of sample size. It would be an overclaim to say simple models beat flexible ones, that the super learner "failed", or that the case study shows a TMLE advantage. The case study has no known truth (`simulation/PROPOSAL-tmle-comparison-2026-10-20.md:13`), and the simulated outcome models are near-logistic in a smooth index, which favours main terms (spec 2026-10-18, line 148).

## How to present it

The strongest honest message is not an estimate. It is that, before any estimate from this analysis existed, the design stage decided, refused and changed things that the unblinded analysis had decided later and with estimates in view. The display and the text should be organized around those three verbs.

It decided the comparison (by rule), the estimand (the ATE for the comparison between operators), the adjustment set (covariates fixed before transport, with arrival state in a sensitivity analysis), complete case as the primary handling of missing outcomes with the loss by arm reported (`final_operators_treated_six_month_not_recorded` and `final_operators_comparator_six_month_not_recorded`), the estimator (a main-terms outcome regression, truncation 0.05) and the tolerances. It refused all four estimands for the comparison between operators at four months, the ATT between operators in the final cohort (the main-terms half-width, `final_operators_sim_main_terms_att_halfwidth_max`, exceeds 0.055 by 0.0001, and the verdict rests on `final_operators_sim_datasets_att` datasets, so say so), every estimand below the ATE in three comparisons, and the two negative controls with too few events (`final_operators_nc_inestimable`). It changed the items in the table that follows, each of which the inventory dates and codes for whether estimates existed (`deviations_inventory.csv`, columns `first_change_date` and `estimates_at_first_change`).

| Decision | Unblinded analysis (inventory row, date, estimates) | Re-execution |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmatory comparison | Row 1, 1 April 2026, available | Rule in the plan; outcome-free evidence in the exported tables |
| Estimand | Row 2, 4 April 2026, available (protocol ATT replaced by ATE) | Inherited: ATE first in the ladder as a plan choice, not a finding |
| Overlap thresholds | Row 21, 1 September 2026, cited | Grades and limits fixed before the design reports existed |
| Full ATE of the protocol comparison | Row 22, 7 October 2026, cited | Pending the simulation verdicts; estimands below the ATE refused |
| Arrival state | Row 14, removed 9 October and re-added 13 October 2026, available then cited | Primary set excludes it; the sensitivity set adds it |
| Library, seed, truncation | Rows 20, 25, 26, August to October 2026, cited | Fixed seed in the plan; library and truncation chosen by simulation |
| Missing outcomes | Row 17, 9 October 2026, cited | Complete case primary (a weaker estimator), longitudinal TMLE as sensitivity |
| Deaths before arrival | Row 28, 30 August 2026, cited or available | Two-sided bias analysis and restriction, written into the Methods before outcomes |

Present the costs in the same plain voice, because they are what make the demonstration believable. The question narrowed from Rescue.Co versus all other transport to Rescue.Co versus other ambulance operators. Complete case is a weaker answer to missing outcomes than the unblinded analysis gave, because deaths at disposition are always recorded and only survivors can be lost (`manuscript/methods.qmd:205`), and the loss differs by arm (`final_operators_treated_six_month_not_recorded` against `final_operators_comparator_six_month_not_recorded`). The negative controls are inconclusive. And the expected precision is about the size of the smallest important difference.

The inventory's count of 18 departures that a design stage "could have raised" assumed the fuller set of seven planned package items (`deviations_inventory.md:33-51`). Release 0.5.0 contains only the first of them, and the other checks were done by the case-study scripts. Recompute the count from the `check_status` column against what 0.5.0 and the scripts actually did before putting any figure like "18 of 34" in the paper.

On governance, say what the spec says: Andrew approves each design report himself and every approver had seen the unblinded results, so the case study is a demonstration of the software and not of clean-room governance (spec 2026-10-18, line 129; `writing/brief.md:32`). Use the word blinding for what was done to the software ("the outcome was withheld from the software"), not for the analysts. Because the ordering evidence is git history and file times that anyone with the repository can rewrite, either state that plainly or add an outside time stamp that costs no governance: archive the repository with the lock and design report hashes before running script 24. That reopens part of the decision against Open Science Framework (OSF) registration, so it is Andrew's call.

Avoid coined labels in reader-facing text. "Population gate", `dossier`, "lock", the verdict labels and the comparison codes are package or project labels, and Andrew has already objected to `GO`, `FLAG` and `STOP` and to "lock hash" (project memory, writing-style note). Describe each by what it does, and keep package terms for the places where they are the interface.

To compare with the unblinded analysis without letting its estimates in, work in four steps. First, keep the unblinded estimates out of every input to scripts 20 to 25 and every justification of a threshold; the only unblinded numbers now in the design stage are linkage and arm counts used as parity checks (`20_steward_indicators.R:29-30`, `21_build_design_data.R:76-78`) and the benchmark overlap shares in `reexec_vs_unblinded_2026-10-20.md:15`, which are outcome-free but not independent, so disclose them. Second, show the decision table above, which uses dates and codes, not estimates. Third, after unblinding, commit and hash the re-execution's own estimates, and only then open the unblinded ones. Fourth, reconcile through a bridge: the re-execution's primary estimate, then the same estimate with arrival state added (the sensitivity lock), then the longitudinal TMLE, then the unblinded analysis's specification, so that each difference is attributed to a named decision. Do not describe agreement as validation or disagreement as refutation; neither analysis has a known truth.

## Risks of overclaiming

| Tempting claim | Why it fails | Safer wording |
|---|---|---|
| The design stage would have caught the problem in 2024 | Later data cut, 2026 thresholds, a different propensity model, analysts had seen estimates | On the data as later cut, the protocol's comparison grades poor for the ATE at the protocol's checkpoint |
| The protocol's comparison cannot be estimated | The Rescue.Co arm is well supported; the ATT was refused by the plan's target | The ATE for the whole cohort rests on a comparison arm that lies largely outside the band |
