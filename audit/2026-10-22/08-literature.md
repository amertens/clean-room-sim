# Recent work on clean-room design and outcome-blind workflows in real-world evidence

Survey for the cleanTMLE audit of 22 October 2026. The survey agent could not write this file (its file tool refused report files), so the main session wrote it from the agent's returned text, recast into paragraphs. The search logs, the full publication list (78 rows), the Crossref verification log and the extracted guidance text are in `audit/2026-10-22/lit/`. Five DOIs were spot-checked against `lit/verify-log.jsonl` and every key cited below was checked against `writing/references.bib`.

## 1. Overall finding

The literature already supplies most parts of cleanTMLE and none of the whole. Staging with a clean room and a masked review team is described [@muntner2024]. Gating the release of results on prespecified diagnostics is established and in use at scale: the Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics (OHDSI) objective diagnostics (Conover 2025) are applied before unblinding in the ASSURE pharmacovigilance program, where 26 of 110 evaluations failed the diagnostics and were not unblinded (Haynes 2026). Outcome-blind simulation on a study's own data is established for targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE), to compare candidate estimators and finalize the statistical analysis plan [@nanceCausalRoadmapSimulations2024a; @gruber2022], and a trial version prespecifies the plan and unblinds in real time (Balzer 2026). A Shiny app compares estimators by plasmode simulation in the analyst's own data (Meng, REFINE2). Regulators and registries ask for a prespecified protocol, estimand and feasibility assessment: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 2024 draft guidance on non-interventional studies, the European Medicines Agency (EMA) 2025 reflection paper, International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) guideline M14, the HARPER protocol template [@wangHARmonizedProtocolTemplate2023] and the Real-World Evidence Registry on the Open Science Framework (OSF). None of them asks for a simulation of whether the estimand can be estimated.

What the literature lacks is three things together: a simulation that decides which estimand a design can deliver before the outcome is seen; a prespecified order of estimands that turns that verdict into a primary estimand; and software that holds the plan, the estimator and the outcome behind a hash and an approval step. The searches found no paper that does this and no package that locks a plan or an estimator before outcomes. The OHDSI gates withhold the effect estimate, not the outcome, since they read observed event counts and negative-control estimates. The estimand-selection papers (Barnard 2026; Liu 2025; Cafri 2025) choose by design-based metrics or by argument, not by a simulation that generates outcomes.

Two results bound the claim. Hiding outcomes does not prevent bias when outcomes are predictable from covariates (Fisher 2020). A plasmode simulation can refute an estimator but cannot certify it (Karim 2026).

For the manuscript, the broad claim that running the causal roadmap with TMLE inside a clean room is itself an advance is not supported by this literature, because each piece has a source. The defensible advance is narrower: outcome-blind simulation extended from the choice of the estimator to the choice of the estimand; the prespecified order that makes the verdict a decision; simulation evidence on how often the verdict is right, and on coverage when the estimand is chosen before rather than after the outcome; and a package that makes the sequence checkable.

## 4. What each strand shows

### (a) Clean room and staging

Muntner 2024 is the anchor. It describes staging, meaning sequential preliminary analyses of population size and potential bias before the comparative analysis, and a clean room with restricted access to data and preliminary results, a deviation policy and an audit trail, overseen by a masked review team that recommends proceeding, adding analyses or stopping. Its four stages are data source and protocol, covariate balance, residual confounding by negative-control outcomes, and the comparative analysis. cleanTMLE adds an estimability simulation between the balance and negative-control stages and exports the construct's Table S1 columns. Later work is thin. Of the 11 works citing Muntner 2024 in OpenAlex, five concern practice or transparency: a review of principled pharmacoepidemiology (Hernandez 2025), a practical guide to registrational real-world evidence (Mayne 2026), an applied cohort study that ran negative-control outcomes before the comparative analysis (Jin 2026), and two transparency editorials that were not read (Wang and Pottegård 2025; Pottegård 2025). None extends the construct methodologically or provides software. A conference abstract describes a Clean Room Committee (Rathnayaka 2024). The one large operating example is ASSURE.

### (b) Outcome-blind design and blind analysis

The principle is old and well stated: design the comparison without looking at outcomes (Rubin 2007; [@rubin2008]), and carry the trial protocol over to observational studies (Small 2024). Blind analysis is routine in parts of physics (Klein 2005) and has been proposed for biology and the social sciences (MacCoun 2015). The closest analogue of a clean room outside pharmacoepidemiology is the Explore and Confirm Analysis Workflow (Thibault 2023), in which analysts see a subset of a cohort, register a script and then receive the full data. Fisher 2020 is the important caution. It shows, analytically and by simulation, that hiding outcomes while choosing matching or propensity-score criteria does not prevent bias when outcomes can be predicted from baseline covariates. cleanTMLE's design stage uses the covariates and treatment of the patients who are later analysed, both to define the estimand (the trimmed population depends on the estimated score) and to choose the estimator. Outcome-blindness removes selection on the outcome; it does not remove selection on chance patterns in the covariates, so the manuscript should say what the blinding buys. The simulation's claim about coverage when the estimand is chosen before rather than after the outcome is the place to test this, and a scenario with a strongly prognostic covariate set would cover Fisher's mechanism. Aikens 2020 takes the opposite route and spends a pilot subset, with its outcomes, in the design phase, which is not compatible with withholding the outcome.

### (c) Prespecification, protocols, registration and guidance

Every source asks for a prespecified protocol, and none asks for a simulation of estimability. HARPER is the protocol template for hypothesis-evaluating treatment-effect studies, STaRT-RWE its companion, and TARGET (2025) the reporting guideline for studies that emulate a target trial; all three are in the bibliography. The FDA draft guidance of March 2024 lists, for the statistical analysis plan, the estimand and an assessment of feasibility with anticipated operating characteristics. The EMA reflection paper of March 2025 asks for confounding methods to be prespecified, for the study and protocol to be registered in the HMA-EMA Catalogue (which replaced the EU PAS Register in February 2024), and for the analysis code to be public. ICH M14 reached Step 4 on 4 September 2025 and takes effect in the EU on 18 March 2026; it says the design, data, methods and analysis should be prespecified after the feasibility assessments, but its feasibility concerns data sources. The OSF Real-World Evidence Registry accepts a protocol and returns a URL or DOI, and its page states no time-stamp rule. Practice lags the guidance: in 41 articles in a population-data journal from 2019 to October 2024, none cited a preregistered protocol (Liu 2026), and in registered cohort studies the primary outcome changed in 48% of publications (Song 2026, already in the bibliography). The American Heart Association scientific statement says a formal protocol should precede every observational study (Mac Grory 2026). Tool support for the design phase is thin: a review found 24 tools for target trial work, 3 of them for design and none covering a whole study (van Hal 2025).

### (d) Replication and benchmarking

RCT-DUPLICATE is the model of judging the design before the results. In the 32-trial report all protocols were registered on ClinicalTrials.gov after the feasibility checks and before any treatment-stratified outcome counts, and agreement with the trials was higher in the closely matched subset (Wang 2023). A 2026 meta-analysis of 107 pairs found a correlation of 0.59, and 0.83 in the 63 pairs whose design matched the trial more closely (Wang 2026). The lesson is indirect: fixing the design before the results is what makes a benchmark readable, and agreement depends on how closely the design matches its target, which argues for settling the estimand early. One result cuts the other way for outcome-blind tuning. In 15 trial emulations, outcome-adaptive LASSO agreed with the trials better than investigator-specified adjustment, and models tuned for treatment prediction alone did poorly (Weckstein 2026). cleanTMLE locks the library and truncation before the outcome but fits the outcome regression to the real outcome afterward, so the point bears on the choice of library, not on the fit.

### (e) Software and platforms

OHDSI is the closest platform. CohortMethod computes equipoise, covariate balance, the minimum detectable relative risk, the expected absolute systematic error and a generalizability measure, each with a threshold set by `createCmDiagnosticThresholds()` (0.2 for equipoise, 0.1 for balance, 10 for the minimum detectable relative risk, 0.25 for the systematic error, and effectively off for generalizability). Each diagnostic gets a pass, warning or fail, and the results table carries an `unblind` flag. Strategus builds and executes a JSON analysis specification across databases, and its reference index lists no function that hashes or locks the specification.

cleanTMLE differs from OHDSI in six ways. The OHDSI gate withholds the effect estimate, but the minimum detectable relative risk uses the observed number of outcome events and the systematic error uses negative-control outcomes, whereas cleanTMLE's design objects contain no outcome and `unblind()` is the one place it enters. The OHDSI thresholds judge the study as designed and are generic, whereas cleanTMLE judges, for each estimand, whether some candidate meets bias, coverage and half-width tolerances in a simulation on the study's own covariates. The OHDSI systematic error pools many negative controls and supports empirical calibration, which needs far more controls than one study usually has, whereas cleanTMLE grades each control against a null band and returns GO, FLAG or STOP per estimand. Strategus specifications are not hashed as far as its index shows, whereas the cleanTMLE lock hashes the plan, the data and the dossier. CohortMethod adjusts by propensity-score matching, stratification or weighting, and its documentation shows no TMLE option. And OHDSI runs many questions through one pipeline, whereas cleanTMLE locks one study.

Sentinel ran a workgroup comparing targeted learning with its propensity-score tools; the page says the work is complete but names no tool or report, and no released Sentinel TMLE tool was found. For the simulation step, REFINE2 is the nearest tool (Meng). It runs a plasmode simulation on the analyst's data to compare estimators for a prespecified ATE, but it does not choose the estimand, it uses outcome models the user specifies rather than a prespecified family, and its repository README asks for a file with an outcome column, so whether it can run without outcomes is unconfirmed. Karim 2026 argues that a plasmode benchmark can refute but not certify, and that regenerating treatment from a bounded propensity score avoids the overlap failures that resampling observed treatments creates, which fits the choice cleanTMLE makes. No R or Python package was found that locks an analysis plan or an estimator before outcomes.

### (f) Choosing the estimator or learner before the outcome

Nance 2024 is the nearest published statement of the design stage: simulations mimic the study's data structure and run after data collection but before any effect is estimated, to compare candidate estimators and finalize the analysis plan, with the estimand named first. Gruber 2022 (the analysis plan), Williamson 2023 and Dang 2023 are in the bibliography. Balzer 2026 gives the trial version: sample splitting picks the outcome regression and treatment model that minimize the cross-validated variance estimate, the plan is tested with parametric and plasmode simulations, and the analysis is unblinded in real time. The two approaches differ in what they read. Balzer's selection uses the outcome within the primary data through sample splitting; cleanTMLE's uses simulated outcomes and no real outcome until approval. Phillips 2023 gives step-by-step choices for a prespecified super learner and calls the approach entirely prespecified, which supports describing a locked library as prespecified while its fit stays data-adaptive. Gruber 2022 in the American Journal of Epidemiology sets the truncation bound as a function of the sample size, so it needs no outcome.

### (g) Anything from 2025 or 2026 that anticipates the claim

The searches found no 2025 or 2026 publication that does what the manuscript claims as its contribution. Three items come close. Balzer 2026 has the workflow, but in randomized trials and for the estimator. REFINE2 has the plasmode comparison, but for a fixed ATE and without a lock. The PHUSE working-group event of 29 January 2026 describes outcome-blind simulations to choose the analysis plan and a Sentinel and FDA project; it is unpublished, the slides could not be read, and it is the item most likely to become a competing paper. Two estimand-selection papers are adjacent: Barnard 2026 proposes design-based metrics and a selection procedure, and Liu 2025 gives a tutorial with an R package, but neither generates outcomes from the design. Cafri 2025 argues for the ATT as the default estimand in drug-safety studies, which bears on the order of estimands. This statement is bounded by the logged searches `g_compete`, `g_estimand_choice`, `g_gate`, `g_lock`, `g_outcomeblind_est`, `g_arxiv3`, `f_plasmode`, `f_plasmode_s2` and `b_blind`, by the cited-by lists, and by web searches on estimand feasibility, plan locking and clean-room software, with the limits in section 2.

## 5. Should it enter the package?

The package should stay near the size of causalRisk, with planned work on `WISHLIST.md`. The survey recommends four small changes and no new exports.

### (a) Clean room and staging: cite only

The package already follows Muntner 2024: `export_design_log(format = "muntner")` in `cleanTMLE/R/design_log.R` writes the Table S1 columns, and `analysis_plan(roles = )` in `cleanTMLE/R/plan.R` records who holds each role.

### (b) Outcome-blind design: adapt, as text only

Add one plain sentence on what outcome-blindness does not guarantee, namely that selection on covariates can still bias the effect when the outcome is predictable from them (Fisher 2020). It belongs in the tamper-evidence paragraph of `cleanTMLE/README.md` and in the notes that `design_report()` writes in `cleanTMLE/R/dossier.R`. The pilot-subset design (Aikens 2020) needs outcomes in the design phase and should be ignored.

### (c) Protocols and registration: adapt, one format option, or defer

A `format = "registration"` option in `export_design_log()` could write a plain-text block with the lock and dossier hashes, the estimands in order, the tolerances, learner libraries and truncations, the negative controls, the roles and the dates, ready to paste into a free-text registration field. This is the registration string for the lock that the wishlist already lists, and the survey estimates about 40 lines and one test. A HARPER exporter should not be built: HARPER's form could not be opened, the registry's field list is unknown, and the paper's governance paragraph says the demonstration has no registration. One sentence in the dossier text (`cleanTMLE/R/dossier.R`) could say that the simulation tables are the feasibility and operating-characteristics element the FDA draft asks for. If the package must stay smaller, the format option waits on the wishlist.

### (d) Replication and benchmarking: ignore for the package

RCT-DUPLICATE needs claims data and trial benchmarks; cite it in the manuscript.

### (e) Software and platforms: adapt one item, ignore four

The item to adapt is the share of patients with a preference score between 0.3 and 0.7 (equipoise), reported by `assess_design()` (`cleanTMLE/R/assess_design.R`) beside `share_outside_band`, carried into the dossier and the JSON list of design fields in `cleanTMLE/R/design_log.R`, and not used as a gate. The paper's simulation already computes it as a comparator (`simulation/PLAN.md`, line 22), so a package function avoids a second implementation. The minimum detectable relative risk should be ignored, since the package forecasts events and half-width from simulated outcomes and needs no outcome count. The expected absolute systematic error and empirical calibration should be ignored, since they need many negative controls. A balance threshold as a gate should be ignored, since standard balance diagnostics are unreliable in small samples (Vereijken 2026; Hripcsak 2025); the package reports the largest standardized difference without gating on it, which is right. A Strategus-style export should be ignored, since it uses a different data model. For the plasmode generator, Karim 2026 should be read in full, and then the construction of `g_world` in `assess_design()` checked to see whether the regenerated treatment is bounded; if it is not, the dossier could record the share of `g_world` near 0 and 1. For the open learner-defaults item, Phillips 2023, Wyss 2024 and Wyss 2026 can be cited in the documentation of `cleanTMLE/R/learners.R`.
