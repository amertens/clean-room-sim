# Support and estimability plan (September 2026)

Status, end of the first build day (2026-09-13): items C, A, B, D, E, F and
G are implemented with tests (cleanTMLE 0.2.0, suite at 634 passing before
the day's last additions), the simplification pass and the documentation
rebuild are in, and the rescueCo multi-arm pipeline (scripts 10 to 14) is
built and running: the cohort and the 90-column design matrix reproduced the
main pipeline exactly, the support gates match on every contrast (C1 SEVERE,
C2 SEVERE, C3 FLAG, C4 FLAG, PRIMARY PASS), and the unadjusted
negative-control ladder reproduces the restriction finding to four decimals.
Support simulations and ladder estimation run checkpointed in the
background. Of the ranked extras, the TARGET emulation-table exporter and
the pre-registered bias-to-null floor are in; the gate
operating-characteristics study, the Lepski truncation rule, the
lock-by-reference option and the incremental-propensity estimator remain
open, in that order.

Purpose of this revision, in one sentence: make cleanTMLE the package that
decides, before outcome access, whether a comparison is estimable and with
which estimand, and strip away everything that distracts from that.

This document holds the reconciliation findings, a critical evaluation of the
package as it stands at 0.1.5, the design for each build item, further ideas
with costs and tests, and the ranking that orders the work. Companion research
memos on the clean-room literature and the software landscape are being
written to `docs/revision/research_clean_room_lit.md` and
`docs/revision/research_tte_software.md`.

## 1. Reconciliation findings

The full record is `rescueCo/results/reconciliation_2026-09.md` and the
machine-readable `reconciliation_table.csv` (regenerated by
`rescueCo/scripts/build_reconciliation_table.R`). The short version: where the
two pipelines estimate the same quantity they agree (matched ATT on the
severe-disability scale -0.021 vs -0.018; negative controls null on the
primary cohort in both; the proportional-odds and survival conclusions match
since July). But 242 of 255 rows are missing from the clean room, because the
case study restricted to ambulance patients at Stage 1 and never faced the
overlap problem that now defines the study. The main analysis's central result
is that the protocol contrast's ATE is not estimable (41 percent of g outside
[0.05, 0.95], maximum weight 304, an untrimmed time-to-arrival estimate of
+14,637 minutes with a narrow interval), while the complete-case ATT (-215
[-265, -165]), the ATO (-221 [-274, -151]) and the trimmed ATE (-275 [-320,
-231]) agree with each other. The clean room has no ATT, no ATO, no trimming,
no graded support verdict, and no estimand ladder, so it cannot express any of
that. Five concrete defects also surfaced: the observed-treatment plasmode
baseline (Shaw et al. 2025), the constant-effect synthetic outcome that makes
all estimands coincide, the 3-replicate feasibility run shipped as an
artifact with coverage 1.000 in every cell, the negative-control re-attach
dead code that silently dropped three of five registered controls, and the
recorded STOP at Check Point 1 followed by estimation with nothing logged
between.

## 2. Critical evaluation of cleanTMLE 0.1.5

The estimation core is sound. The native modular TMLE agrees with tmle::tmle
to 0.01 on the risk-difference scale under test, the suite is green (502
passing), and the causalRisk-style cumulative-risk grammar (specify_models,
identify_*, estimate_*risk, make_table1/2, plot methods) is clean, fully
decoupled from governance, and already validated against causalRisk's ACTG 320
analysis.

Everything around that core has grown faster than its value. The package
exports 132 functions plus 45 S3 methods for a tested scope of one design
(binary point treatment, binary outcome, risk difference). The single largest
export category is governance: 38 functions for locks, audits, checkpoints,
decision logs, thresholds, masking and authorization. Sixteen exports have no
caller anywhere outside the package's own tests, and four have no caller at
all. There are two decision-log implementations that do not talk to each
other. `run_positivity_diagnostics()` demands a class that no constructor in
the package produces, so it cannot accept the package's own propensity fits.
`plot_dq_heatmap()` is exported, undocumented and never called.

The strongest evidence against the enforcement layer is the package's own
case study. The 0.1.5 release hardened outcome authorization into
tamper-evident tokens with audit fingerprints, and the rescueCo pipeline
bypasses every one of those checks: Stage 2 calls
`unmask_outcome(allow_unauthorized = TRUE)` for an outcome it does not use,
Stage 4 wraps the authorization assertion in a tryCatch that only logs, and
the shipped results were produced across two recorded STOPs. When the
software's sole sophisticated user routes around the enforcement at every
opportunity, the enforcement is friction, not protection. What actually
protected the analysis in the parallel main pipeline was statistical: the
overlap grades, the implausibility guard, the negative-control ladder, and
the discipline of computing every reported number from a rerun. The package
should invest there.

The simplification therefore has one principle: cleanTMLE provides
estimation, support assessment, outcome-blind simulation, and reporting;
process documentation lives in cleanroomGov or in the analyst's hands; and
software-enforced authorization is retired. Masking stays, because
`mask_outcome()` is the one honest blinding device (the outcome column is
physically absent), and a lock stays, because sealing the specification and
the seed is what makes the design stage reproducible. Hash-bound gate tokens
go.

## 3. The 0.2.0 architecture

The workflow becomes five verbs, in the causalRisk pattern of specify, then
estimate, then report, with two design-stage verbs inserted between
specification and estimation because that insertion is the package's reason
to exist:

    lock <- create_analysis_lock(data, treatment, outcome, covariates, ...)
    ps   <- fit_ps(lock, method = "superlearner")           # or glm, external
    sup  <- assess_support(ps)                              # verdict + plots
    fea  <- estimand_feasibility(ps)                        # per-estimand gate
    lad  <- declare_estimand_ladder(lock, primary = "ATE",
                                    fallbacks = c("trimmed_ATE", "ATT", "ATO"))
    sim  <- simulate_support(lock, ps)                      # outcome-blind map
    fit  <- run_estimand_ladder(lock, ps, ladder = lad)     # Stage 4
    design_report(lock, sup, fea, sim)                      # the dossier

Every object prints a small readable summary and plots itself. The verdict
vocabulary is the main pipeline's PASS, FLAG, SEVERE, FAIL, anchored to
Muntner et al.'s review-team decisions (proceed, additional analyses,
terminate) rather than the invented GO/FLAG/STOP triple.

### Item C, first: fix the outcome-blind simulation

`simulate_support(lock, ps_fit, surface, estimands, estimators, reps)`
replaces the baseline of both plasmode entry points. Design: resample W rows
with replacement, draw A from the fitted propensity evaluated at the resampled
W (optionally amplified as the near-positivity scenario already does), draw Y
from a prespecified synthetic outcome family. The observed-treatment design
survives only as `design = "sample_treatment"` with a deprecation warning
citing Shaw et al. (2025). The synthetic family
`support_surfaces(confounding = c(0, 1, 2), modification = c(0, 0.5, 1),
complexity = c("linear", "interactions"))` builds the outcome on the linear
predictor of the fitted propensity, so outcome dependence points along
exactly the covariate direction that drives non-overlap, adds treatment-effect
modification along the same score so ATE, ATT and ATO genuinely differ, and
optionally adds interactions a misspecified Q will miss. Truth for every
estimand is computed on each replicate from the generating model (ATT truth
weights the effect by the drawn A; ATO truth weights by g(1-g); trimmed truth
restricts to the band). Output is bias, RMSE, coverage and SE calibration per
estimator per estimand per grid point, plus the support map: the largest
confounding and modification strength at which each estimator stays within a
bias tolerance with near-nominal coverage. `run_plasmode_feasibility()` and
`run_plasmode_dq_stress()` keep their interfaces but gain
`design = c("generate_treatment", "sample_treatment")` with the new default,
and results carry the design in their metadata. Runs below 50 replicates are
tagged demonstration-only and force a FLAG (TODO A.15). Petersen et al.'s
parametric bootstrap ships as `check_locked_estimator()`, labelled optimistic
because the fitted Q generates the truth. Tests: on a DGP with known truth and
a built-in severe violation, the generate-treatment design shows the ATE
estimator failing and the ATT and ATO estimators standing, while the
sample-treatment design is shown mislabelling the healthy cohort as biased
(the Shaw et al. artifact reproduced in miniature).

### Item A: assess_support()

One object that absorbs `compute_ps_diagnostics()`, the broken
`run_positivity_diagnostics()`, and the main pipeline's `pos_diagnostics()`
plus `near_deterministic()`. Fields: n by arm, g range, counts and percent
outside the prespecified band, max and 99th percentile IPTW weight, mean
weight, Kish ESS by arm, propensity c-statistic, the graded verdict with the
main pipeline's thresholds as defaults (FLAG above 1 percent outside or max
weight 30, SEVERE above 25 percent or 150, FAIL above 60 percent or 1000),
the caveat string that travels with every downstream estimate, the
near-deterministic strata table (binary covariates whose on stratum has at
least 20 members, fewer than min_treated treated, and treated proportion
outside [0.10, 0.90]), the escalation rule (conditional non-overlap raises
PASS or FLAG to SEVERE, never to FAIL, and never downward), and an optional
rpart-based search for multivariate violation regions in the spirit of the
PoRT algorithm (RISCA is not installed here; a compact native implementation
with max depth 3 and a minimum stratum size reports subgroup, size, treated
share). `print()` gives the one-paragraph verdict; `plot()` draws the
mirrored density of g by arm with the band and trimmed region shaded, the
idiom halfmoon and cobalt users already read. Thresholds come from
`support_thresholds()`, a plain list constructor with the defaults above.

### Item B: estimand feasibility and the ladder

`estimand_feasibility(ps_fit, estimands, trim_levels)` returns one row per
estimand: the relevant weight function's maximum and 99th percentile (ATE
weights 1/g and 1/(1-g); ATT control weights g/(1-g) bounded by max g;
ATC mirrored; ATO weights bounded by 1 by construction; trimmed ATE at each
level after refitting is approximated at assessment time by the trimmed
band's diagnostics with refit deferred to estimation), ESS by arm under that
weight, the number removed or down-weighted, and the verdict from the same
graded rule applied to that estimand's implied weights. `who_is_unsupported(
ps_fit, vars)` reproduces script 54: standardized differences of removed vs
kept on a user-supplied list of interpretable variables, overall and by arm.
`declare_estimand_ladder(lock, primary, fallbacks, trigger = "SEVERE")`
records the switching rule on the lock before outcomes are unlocked; it is
TODO A.14 made concrete. `run_estimand_ladder(lock, ps_fit, ladder)` runs the
primary if feasible plus every feasible fallback, labels each row with
estimand, verdict, caveat and the implausibility flags, and refuses an
infeasible primary unless `override_reason` is given, which is then recorded
in the lock's design log. Tests: a simulated severe-overlap DGP where the
ladder must skip the ATE and land on the ATT, with the truth known for both.

### Item D: estimators

`run_att_tmle(lock, ps_fit)`: complete-case ATT via tmle::tmle with
evalATT, never through Delta, estimand named
E[Y(1)-Y(0) | A = 1, outcome observed], and a test asserting the g
specification (library, truncation, prescreen setting) is byte-identical to
the ATE fit's, the prescreenW.g lesson. `estimate_ato(lock, ps_fit)`: the
augmented overlap-weighted estimator of Mao, Li and Greene (2019) with the
influence-function variance and the exact-balance check reported (PSweight is
not installed; implemented natively, tested against the closed-form Hajek
estimator when augmentation is switched off and against known truth under
randomization). `run_trimmed_tmle(lock, ps_fit, levels = c(0.05, 0.10),
rule = c("fixed", "crump"))`: trims, refits g on the trimmed subset, records
dropped counts by arm, falls back to the next level when the refit gate does
not clear, and offers Crump et al.'s data-adaptive threshold.
`resolve_truncation_rule()` gains `"lepski"` (the Xu, Gruber and van der Laan
adaptive rule) beside `sqrt_n_ln_n` variants with c = 5 or 6.
`implausibility_check(est, y, a, family)` ports the main pipeline's guard
(sign against crude, five times crude, beyond the largest arm rate, beyond
the observed range, with the noise floors) and is attached to every estimate
the package reports. `run_ipcw_tmle()` gains `prescreenW.g = FALSE` as the
default on its delegation path, the named gbound rule, and seed averaging
with the Rubin-style combination from tmle_seed_avg(). CV-TMLE (cvQinit and
cross-fitted g) becomes the recommendation printed under FLAG or worse,
citing Smith et al. (2025). Incremental propensity interventions (Kennedy
2019) and modified treatment policies (estimate_lmtp exists) are documented
as the positivity-free rungs the ladder can end on; a native incremental
estimator is deferred (see rankings).

### Item E: multi-contrast locks

`create_contrast_locks(data, treatment_factor, contrasts, covariates, ...)`
builds one lock per contrast from a multi-level treatment with shared
covariate construction and shared negative controls, returning a named list
with a combined print method. The Rescue.Co transport3 contrasts are the test
case, in miniature via a simulated three-arm DGP.

### Item F: negative controls on the restriction ladder

`run_negative_control_ladder(lock, restrictions)` fits each registered
control on each nested cohort (a named list of row filters) with the
TMLE-based negative-control estimator and reports where a control turns from
failing to null. A control failing on the full cohort and passing after a
restriction is evidence for the restriction; the result carries that reading
so the analyst can record it. Registration happens before any variance
filter, and a registered control that a filter would drop is retained and
flagged instead of dropped (TODO A.16).

### Item G: design-stage diagnostics without the outcome

`check_process_indicators(lock, indicators, condition_on)`: the collider
check from script 46, regressing each care-process indicator on A given the
conditioning set, classifying physiology, process and geography, and
reporting standardized coefficients with the 0.10 threshold. The two-team
audit split and the STOP-override entry type from the original list are
folded into the lighter design log: the log gains an `actor` field and an
explicit `override` type, so a STOP followed by estimation without an
override entry is visible in one line, but no token machinery enforces it.

### Item H: engineering

Per-contrast checkpointing with a resume argument in every long loop (the
script 42 pattern: save after each unit, reload with an environment flag);
guarded stopCluster; one retry of a fresh socket cluster before serial
fallback; `fit_ps(method = "external")` documents the wrap_ps_fit route; and
a lock-by-reference option so six stages do not each serialize the data (the
lock stores the data once on disk and stages hold the path plus the hash).

## 5. Further ideas, with problem, cost and test

Support map versus reality, the headline check. Problem: nothing currently
demonstrates that the outcome-blind machinery predicts anything. On
Rescue.Co the support map should place the protocol-contrast ATE outside
tolerance and the ATT, ATO and trimmed ATE inside it, and the primary
cohort's ATE inside it, before any outcome is unlocked; the case study then
reports predicted versus realized as its headline. Cost: no new machinery,
one table in the report. Test: the case-study rerun itself. Ranked first
among the extras.

Gate operating characteristics. Problem: the PASS/FLAG/SEVERE thresholds are
inherited from one study; their error rates are unknown. Design: simulate
DGPs with graded positivity strain, record the verdict and whether the ATE
was recoverable (bias within tolerance at nominal coverage), and report the
gate's sensitivity and specificity as a function of the overlap statistics.
Cost: a study script and several compute hours, reusing
run_gate_operating_characteristics.R scaffolding. Test: the gate must order
the strain grid monotonically. Ranked second; scheduled after the case study
because it consumes the same compute budget.

Conditional positivity report per stratum. Problem: global overlap statistics
missed the 06b failure; the design team should know which subgroups an
estimand covers. Design: per-covariate-stratum g summaries joined to the
support map, reported as a coverage table. Cost: small, mostly reuses
near-deterministic machinery. Test: a DGP with one deterministic stratum must
be named. Ranked third; the near-deterministic table plus the rpart search
already carry most of the value, so this is a reporting extension.

Bias-curve and E-value thresholds pre-registered. Problem: sensitivity
thresholds computed after unblinding invite motivated reading. Design:
declare_estimand_ladder accepts optional evalue_floor and bias_to_null_floor
recorded on the lock; design_report prints declared against realized. Cost:
trivial. Test: round trip through the lock. Ranked fourth because it is
nearly free.

A TARGET emulation-table exporter. Problem: the reporting guideline for
target trial emulations is now final (Cashin, Hansford, Hernan et al., JAMA
2025, 21 items, with the protocol-versus-emulation parallel columns), and
every field it asks for already lives in the lock, the ladder and the
support assessment. Design: emulation_table(lock) renders the two-column
protocol/emulation table from the recorded specification. Cost: small,
reporting only. Test: field coverage against a filled lock. Ranked between
fourth and fifth; the same OHDSI CohortMethod precedent (effect estimates
withheld until named diagnostics pass) is the published analogue for
design_report's unblinding framing and is cited there.

Time-window confounder and differential-restriction DQ presets (TODO A.11,
A.12), now with real Rescue.Co parameters (strike windows; transfer-pattern
restriction falling 34 percent on one arm and 3.5 on the other). Cost:
moderate; two new degradation functions. Test: each scenario must degrade a
susceptible candidate and spare a robust one. Ranked fifth.

Design-sample mode (Heller, Rosenbaum and Small). Problem: outcome-blind Q0
is the weakest link of the simulation; a held-out split with outcomes visible
to the design team fixes it at a sample-size price. Design: split at Stage 1,
fit Q0 on the split, exclude those rows from Stage 4, report the cost up
front. Cost: moderate, touches the lock. Ranked sixth: valuable but the
Rescue.Co case study cannot spare the events (about 100 deaths), so it would
ship untested on the motivating data; simulate_support's four Q0 sources
already record the choice (negative-control outcome, auxiliary outcome,
held-out split, synthetic) and the split source can land with a simulated
example.

Native incremental propensity interventions (Kennedy 2019). Problem: the
ladder currently ends at ATO; the positivity-free estimand family is a
better terminal rung. Cost: high (new estimator with influence-function
variance). Ranked last for this cycle; estimate_lmtp() already gives a
working positivity-free endpoint, and the documentation points there.

## 6. Order of work

1. Item C, the plasmode design fix, since every downstream selection and gate
   result depends on it, with the Shaw et al. comparison test.
2. Items A and B, assess_support and the feasibility ladder, the package's
   new spine.
3. Item D, the estimators the ladder lands on, with the same-g test.
4. The simplification pass (section 4) and the documentation rewrite: the
   staged-analysis vignette reframed around the five verbs with the Rescue.Co
   overlap story as the running example, and a short "why not a traditional
   clean room" section: a traditional clean room blinds the analyst but
   cannot tell them whether the design supports the estimand; this package's
   design stage produces the support map and the feasibility table that
   answer it, and the estimator that finally runs is doubly robust.
5. Part 1 items 1 to 6: the rescueCo rebuild on all five locks, in
   checkpointed stages ordered mortality and time-to-arrival first so the
   four-row reconciliation table closes early.
6. Items E, F, G, H alongside 5 (the rebuild consumes them), then the
   further-ideas list in ranked order as compute allows.

Constraints observed throughout: one long R job at a time, checkpoints on
anything over an hour, reinstall after every package edit, every new function
tested against a known truth, every changed number in rescueCo/results from a
rerun, and the main analysis read but never modified.
