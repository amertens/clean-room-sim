# Clean-room and main-pipeline reconciliation (2026-09)

Data cut 2026-03-27. This memo records the state of agreement between the
clean-room case study (`clean-room-sim/rescueCo`) and the independent main
analysis (`../rescueCo_analysis`, read only) as of 2026-09-13, before the
September rebuild, and lists the changes that close each gap. It follows the
July memo (`reconciliation_2026-07-28.md`), which aligned the cohort to 1,616
and fixed the survival time grid; those fixes are committed and are the
baseline here.

The machine-readable companion is `reconciliation_table.csv`, one row per
(contrast, cohort, outcome, estimand, estimator), generated by
`rescueCo/scripts/build_reconciliation_table.R` from the CSVs both pipelines
publish. It is regenerated by rerunning that script, never edited. Verdict
counts at the pre-rebuild baseline:

| Verdict | Rows | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| agree | 2 | within a quarter of a main-pipeline SE |
| benign_difference | 2 | both present, difference explained below |
| missing_in_cleanroom | 242 | the main pipeline reports it; the clean room does not yet |
| missing_in_main | 5 | clean-room-only estimator variants on gose_favorable |
| not_comparable | 4 | different quantities by design (proxy survival; inestimable NCs) |

## Where the pipelines already agree

On the one contrast both analyse (Rescue.Co vs other ambulance on the
1,616-patient primary cohort), the comparable rows agree. The clean-room
matched ATT on the GOSE > 4 dichotomy, negated to the severe-disability scale,
is -0.021 against the main pipeline's -0.018 [-0.080, 0.040], within a quarter
of a main-pipeline SE. Both socioeconomic negative controls are null on the
primary cohort in both pipelines (urban household -0.0017 vs -0.0027). The
proportional-odds conclusion matches (not rejected; July memo discrepancy 2),
and the survival estimators sit on the same 2 to 5 percent absolute-risk scale
since the July grid fix.

## Discrepancy 1: the clean room analyses one contrast; the study has five. VERDICT: missing, rebuild adds them.

The main analysis now runs four contrasts on the 8,323-patient known-transport
cohort (C1 Rescue.Co vs everyone else, the protocol comparator; C2 Rescue.Co
vs non-ambulance; C3 any ambulance vs non-ambulance; C4 Rescue.Co vs other
ambulance with transfers included) plus the primary cohort, and its central
finding is about estimability: C1 and C2 have severe practical positivity
violations (41.3 and 39.4 percent of g outside [0.05, 0.95], maximum IPTW
weights 304 and 285) on which the ATE is not estimable, while the ATT among
outcome-observed patients, the trimmed ATE, and the overlap-weighted ATO are.
The clean-room case study restricted to ambulance patients at Stage 1 and so
never faced the overlap problem that is now the study's methodological point.
That is why 242 of 255 reconciliation rows are missing_in_cleanroom.

Change: add locks for the four multi-arm contrasts built from the same
`transport3` rule as `analysis/02_define_exposure_outcomes_covariates.R`, keep
the primary-cohort lock, and give each lock its own support assessment, gate,
estimand ladder, and Stage 4 estimates (Part 1 item 1 of the September plan).
The protocol contrast C1 becomes the case study's presented primary, with the
clean room stopping the ATE on it and re-targeting.

## Discrepancy 2: primary outcome and the Check Point 1 STOP. VERDICT: design flaw in the case study, fixed by adopting the main outcome set.

The clean-room primary is `gose_good` (GOSE > 4), prevalence 0.92 in the
cohort. Check Point 1 read the events-below-minimum rule on that outcome,
recorded STOP on 2026-05-05, and the pipeline proceeded anyway: Stage 4 wraps
`assert_outcome_authorized()` in a `tryCatch` that only logs and then calls
`unmask_outcome(..., allow_unauthorized = TRUE)` with no logged rationale
between the STOP and the estimate (`results/cleanTMLE_audit_trail.csv` rows 3
and 7; `manuscript_artifacts/audit_summary.csv` carries both STOPs to this
day). The main pipeline's primary is six-month mortality, roughly 100 events
in the primary cohort, on which the same checkpoint passes.

Change: adopt the main pipeline's ten outcomes and definitions
(`death_by_6mo` primary; `severe_disability` = GOSE <= 4 replacing
`gose_good` as the functional dichotomy so the rare category is the event;
`gose_favorable`, `gose`, `death_in_hospital`, `transferred_out`,
`transferred_out_higher_care`, and the three time outcomes), make IPCW via
Delta the primary handling of missing follow-up with complete case as the
sensitivity (flipping the current ordering), and regenerate
`case_study_metadata.json` by rerunning. Any surviving override becomes a
logged decision with a rationale, so the audit can never show STOP followed by
estimation with nothing between (Part 1 items 4 and 8).

## Discrepancy 3: nuisance models are much weaker in the clean room. VERDICT: benign in July, scheduled to close now.

The clean room adjusts for 21 covariates with SL.glm + SL.glmnet + SL.mean
(SL.mean took 0.215 of the propensity weight), against the main pipeline's 90
design columns from 39 variables and an 11-entry library (SL.mean, three
glmnet penalties, SL.bayesglm, SL.earth, SL.gam, two PCA-GLM learners, two
screen.corP pairs), 10-fold CV, `prescreenW.g = FALSE`, and the
`5/(sqrt(n) log n)` truncation default. The July memo documented this as one
driver of the GOSE>4 gap (+0.028 clean room vs +0.007 main).

Change: rebuild W from the main pipeline's construction rules
(`COVARIATE_TYPES`, factor expansion with 2 percent rare-level pooling,
missingness indicators above 1 percent, `NEAR_CONSTANT_EXCLUDED`, hour-of-day
sine and cosine, months since start, strike period, injury sub-county, payment
type, and no `hosp_code`, which is an outcome proxy), reproduce the library
via the package's learner builders (adding the PCA-GLM and ridge/enet
learners to cleanTMLE), use 10-fold CV and `prescreenW.g = FALSE`, and record
the fitted SuperLearner weights in the design report, which neither pipeline
currently writes to disk (Part 1 items 2 and 3).

## Discrepancy 4: negative-control attrition and the missing restriction ladder. VERDICT: bug plus missing feature.

Stage 1 registers five negative controls, but three are dropped by the
near-zero-variance filter and the re-attach block tests membership in a matrix
built after the filter, so it can never fire
(`scripts/01_stage1_build_cohort.R`, re-attach block; only two controls appear
in `results/negative_control_results.csv` and no re-attach entry exists in the
decision log). Separately, the clean room runs its controls on one cohort
only. The main pipeline's finding is a ladder result: urban household +6.8 pp
[4.9, 8.7] and wood or charcoal cooking fuel -5.2 pp [-7.4, -2.9] against
Rescue.Co on the full ambulance cohort, both null after the transfer and
prior-care exclusions. Restriction, not adjustment, removed the measurable
confounding, and a single-cohort negative-control step cannot see that.

Change: register controls before any variance filter and never drop them
silently (TODO A.16), and run the controls along the restriction ladder (full
ambulance cohort, transfers excluded, transfers and prior care excluded) with
the package's new ladder function (Part 1 items 2 and 6).

## Discrepancy 5: the plasmode design is the one Shaw et al. warn about. VERDICT: package bug, fixed package-side.

`run_plasmode_feasibility()` and the baseline scenario of
`run_plasmode_dq_stress()` keep every patient's observed treatment and
simulate only the outcome (`A_rep <- A`; `cleanTMLE/R/plasmode_dq.R` line 893
and `cleanroom.R` line 1349 at 0.1.5). Shaw et al. (2025, arXiv:2504.11740)
show this induces a positivity violation by construction, so propensity-based
estimators look biased and undercover even when the source population is
fine. The July DQ artifact shows the symptom's companion problem from the
other direction: the 3-replicate Stage 3 run in
`manuscript_artifacts/table_dq_coverage.csv` reports coverage 1.000 in every
cell, which no one should read as evidence. The synthetic outcome is also a
covariate-only Q0 plus a constant additive effect, under which ATE, ATT and
ATO coincide and a correctly specified Q extrapolates without error, so the
simulation cannot exhibit positivity bias even on C1 where the real ATE was
wrong by a factor of thirty.

Change: switch both plasmode entry points to the generate-treatment design
(resample W, draw A from the fitted propensity, draw Y from the synthetic
outcome model), keep the sample-treatment design only as a deprecated option
citing Shaw et al., give the synthetic outcome family confounding,
effect-modification and complexity axes so the estimands genuinely differ,
and rerun candidate selection and the DQ stress at an inferential replicate
count with a forced demonstration flag below 50 replicates (Part 2 item C;
TODO A.15; Part 1 item 9).

## Discrepancy 6: estimands. VERDICT: missing; the September build's main point.

The clean room estimates one estimand (the ATE) with one fallback comparison
(matched TMLE). The main pipeline reports, on every contrast, the ATE with a
graded overlap verdict attached to every row, the trimmed ATE with a
propensity refit, the complete-case ATT (estimand
E[Y(1)-Y(0) | A = 1, outcome observed], never through Delta because the
package ATT loses double robustness there), the augmented-free Hajek ATO, and
the matched ATT, plus an implausibility guard on each estimate. On C1 those
disagree exactly as the theory predicts: ATE on time to arrival +14,637
minutes [11,594, 17,680] and flagged implausible, ATT -215 [-265, -165],
ATO -221 [-274, -151], trimmed ATE -275 [-320, -231].

Change: implement support assessment, the estimand feasibility table, the
pre-registered estimand ladder, and the ATT, augmented ATO, and
trimmed-with-refit estimators in cleanTMLE with tests, then run all of them
on every rescueCo lock (Part 2 items A, B, D; Part 1 item 5). The
reconciliation table closes when the four-row mortality comparison and the
C1 time-to-arrival contrast reproduce within seed noise.

## Notes carried into the rebuild

The main pipeline is the reference for cohort rules and numbers, and the
following facts from it constrain the rebuild. `tmle_seed_avg()` seeds every
fit with TMLE_SEED = 77777 immediately before the call, uses V.Q = V.g =
V.Delta = 10, cvQinit = TRUE, `prescreenW.g = FALSE` on every call including
the ATT refit (the omission of which once moved the mortality ATT from -2.08
to -1.34 points), and leaves gbound at the package default. Its standalone
propensity fits use V = 5 in scripts 42, 43, 45 and 53 but V = 10 in script
04, an inconsistency to note, not to copy blindly. `available_sl_library()`
accepts a model argument that it ignores; all roles get the identical
library. The ATT block's caveats (complete cases, never through Delta, named
estimand) are load-bearing and are ported as-is. Two open data items belong
in the decision log as pending amendments: Rescue.Co is checking 76 treated
patients in the primary cohort who lack a Rescue.Co ID (dropping them moves
six-month mortality from -0.0225, p = 0.021, to -0.0152, p = 0.083 under a
reduced library), and the team recalls improving Rescue.Co identification
partway through enrolment, so part of the rising Rescue.Co share may be
ascertainment.

## Bottom line

The July fixes hold: cohort, survival grid, and PO test still reconcile. What
remains is not disagreement but absence. The clean room has never faced the
overlap problem that now defines the study, and closing the table means
teaching the package to grade support, choose a feasible estimand before
outcome access, and estimate the full ladder, then rerunning the case study
on all five locks. The reconciliation table is the checklist: every
missing_in_cleanroom row is an estimate the rebuilt pipeline must produce,
and the memo above records the five changes that produce them.

Where both pipelines estimate the same quantity the same way, they agree
within a quarter of a main-pipeline SE: the C4 mortality ATE (-0.0290 clean
room vs -0.0272 main), the C1 and C3 mortality rows, and every negative
control to four decimals. The ten benign rows split into three documented
families. The ATO rows differ by estimator family on purpose (Hajek weights
with a percentile bootstrap in the main pipeline, the augmented
overlap-weighted estimator with influence-function variance here). The
trimmed rows differ in who is trimmed and how g is refit. The primary-cohort
ATE and ATT differ only in nuisance specification (the locked rwe_wide
SuperLearner and seed here versus the main library), at 0.6 to 0.8 of a main
SE with the same sign. Each family's reason is stamped on its rows by the
build script, guarded to one main-pipeline SE so a genuine surprise would
still surface as open.

The design log recorded what the workflow promised it would: two C1
overrides (the ATE estimated only as a labelled reconciliation row, with the
recorded reason), two C2 estimand switches at the SEVERE trigger, and the
pending amendment about the 76 unconfirmed Rescue.Co IDs stamped on every
lock. No estimate anywhere tripped the implausibility guard, and the
time-to-arrival ladder, the outcome that once returned +14,637 minutes,
reports bounded estimands agreeing on 3 to 4.5 hours faster arrival on the
population contrasts and a null within-ambulance difference on the primary
cohort.

The remaining missing_in_cleanroom rows are the main pipeline's wider
outcome and estimator surface (the ordinal GOSE models, the survival
estimators, the subgroup and equity analyses), which the case study does not
reproduce by design. The C2 to C4 support maps completed at 01:39 on
2026-09-14 and extend the design-stage record without changing any estimate
above: C2 mirrors C1 (the ATE fails the mildest surface at coverage 0.87
while the bounded estimands pass), C3 is robust across the grid, and C4
shows marginal mild-surface strain (coverage 0.86 to 0.90 on the unmatched
estimands) consistent with its FLAG verdict.
